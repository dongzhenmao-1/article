Prologue
---------------------------------------------------------

This is not a story about dedicating one's entire life to someone else.

No matter where you go on the road of life, it can only be your own path.

No matter what choice you make, no matter how good you think these things are, or how heavy the burdens you carry,

Everything I have gained in the course of my life belongs to me.

Therefore, as long as my sister can put on a happy smile, I can usually let most things slide with "whatever".

This is the path I was destined to take.

Moreover, it takes a considerable amount of time to discover this.

This almost careless and casual attitude will determine one's entire life.

But even if it were discovered, the entire world would already be fully constructed, making any modifications impossible.

Therefore, we can only continue to live in this way.

This book tells such a story.

I was three years old when my younger sister was born. I still remember that day, and I think I'll never forget her birthday either, since she was born on February 14th.

Record-breaking heavy snow fell in the days surrounding my sister's birth, but the day she was born was exceptionally sunny all day. While the adults were busy dealing with the thick snow on the roads and rooftops, all I worried about was whether the snow on the kindergarten playground would melt. Would I still be able to have a snowball fight with my friends? I thought to myself as I kneaded a snowball without gloves. At that moment, my mind was completely focused on the snow, and even when I got home from school and heard that my sister had been born, I only felt an "Oh—" and didn't care at all.

At that time, I couldn't understand what a younger sister was. Even though the adults said that my sister was related to me by blood, I still couldn't truly understand it. Although I had heard that my sister, like me, was born from my mother's womb, I still didn't understand what that meant because I didn't remember being in my mother's womb.

With one more person living in the house, my room will eventually feel cramped.

At the time, that was all I could understand.

I didn't visit my newborn sister in the hospital because my parents thought it was inappropriate to let a noisy little brat cause trouble there. Six days after her birth, my mother took her home from the hospital, and that's when I finally saw her for the first time. Since I saw my father busy making the bed and preparing various things the day before my mother was discharged, I could understand that my sister was finally coming to our home.

And so, I made my first contact with my little sister, who was being held in my mother's arms. My first impression of her was that she was "delicate and frail." She was even smaller than the toddlers at the kindergarten, with bald patches on her back and rosy cheeks. She was as fragile as a newborn puppy, and seemed to understand her weakness, so she would cry to ask for help from those around her.

To put it bluntly, I don't think my sister and I are the same kind of creature.

Overall, my first impression of my sister wasn't good. Afraid of making a terrible mistake by even touching her, I soon began consciously avoiding her. Years later, though a little unsteady, my sister was finally able to stand on her own two legs, becoming a creature "about the same size as me." But by then, a barrier had formed between us. Although the barrier wasn't thick—a flimsy, easily shattered polystyrene wall—it completely blocked our view, making it impossible to see each other.

All I know about my sister at that age is that she is very afraid of the cold.

Perhaps because she was born on a snowless day, when she was about three years old, her father and we were sledding in the winter when there was a lot of snowfall. We had only been playing for a short while when she immediately started crying, saying, "It's so cold, I want to go home." Although I didn't say it out loud at the time, I thought to myself: What a spineless little thing. To be honest, I didn't expect that even later, that assessment would never change.

Because I didn't know how to deal with this girl who was a whole size younger than me, and because she was closer to our parents than me, and would immediately cry and seek their help when she encountered difficulties, I never really had a chance to step in. I barely even had a few words with her. However, aside from when she was showing weakness, my sister didn't really express her own thoughts, which was probably related to her personality. That's what I always thought. Since we really didn't communicate much, I wasn't sure if that was really the case.

My parents originally planned for my younger sister to live with me in the children's room, but because she's very attached to them, especially my mother, she ended up sleeping with them. I'm glad I still have my own room and hope it stays that way. I think if we shared a room, we'd both feel suffocated, and our relationship might even deteriorate.

At that time, my sister and I probably didn't understand that we were siblings. Siblings should help each other. Although this wasn't a written rule, it was at least what my parents hoped for. Although I sensed my parents' expectations of me, I pretended not to notice; as for my sister, I think she probably didn't understand anything. After all, she was busy surviving and didn't have the extra energy to pay attention to anything else.

In this way, we grew up without building any relationships.

My younger sister started coming to me crying and telling me things when she was six and I was ten.

It was late August, the tail end of summer vacation. I could feel the sun setting a little earlier, but more depressing than that was the fact that "school is about to start." Time seems to evaporate as quickly as it always has, like water in a swimming pool. The idea that summer vacation was over must be a lie, right? But looking at my arms, my skin did indeed have sunburn marks. And all I could do was sigh at the unyielding evidence.

As I stood directly in front of the fan, scratching the spot where I'd been bitten by a mosquito, I felt a faint breath behind me. I turned around and saw my sister standing there. Although I didn't make a sound, I was actually quite shocked, so surprised that I even forgot the itchy feeling of the mosquito bite.

My younger sister, unusually so, approached me, holding a drawing diary. "Help me..." she whispered, handing me the diary hesitantly as our eyes met. Hearing this request, a bad feeling crept into my heart, a feeling that became a stark reality when I inadvertently opened the diary.

There was almost no record of this summer in the notebook. Wow—I touched the snow-white pages and exclaimed in amazement.

Not only were the pictures and text blank, but even the dates were completely empty. I looked at my sister through the diary; she was gazing at me with teary eyes. In terms of our positions, I was sitting and she was standing, so she was looking down at me, but for some reason, I had the illusion of looking down on her. This seemed to be the first time I realized how much younger my sister was than me.

"Summer homework?"

I asked, and my sister nodded slightly. I remembered writing something similar when I was in lower grades. For assignments like drawing journals, no matter what excuse you made, you'd get scolded if you didn't finish, and you couldn't ask your parents for help. I understood why my sister had come to me.

Except for the first three days, the entire diary was blank, giving me a real understanding of the saying "fishing for three days and drying nets for two." I scratched my head in frustration. Although I understood why my sister had come to me crying, even if she asked for my help, I was powerless to do so. I couldn't even remember how I spent my summer vacation, let alone how my sister had spent those days.

What did you do all summer?

I didn't mean to blame her; I was simply curious about how my younger sister was spending her summer vacation. Was she engrossed in something that made her reluctant to set aside time for writing in her diary? That's what I wanted to ask. But to my sister, it probably sounded like I was blaming her, and tears started welling up in her eyes.

"Oh dear!" I panicked; this was bad. Seeing my sister's lips twitching and her face on the verge of tears, cold sweat poured down my back. It wouldn't be good if my mother, who was in another room, heard her crying and I got scolded. I quickly pushed my sister, who was about to burst into tears, away from the living room. Although the fan was still running, I didn't have the energy to turn it off.

"Don't cry, don't cry." After we got to the room on the second floor, I tried my best to comfort my sister. She also tried to sniffle and hold back her tears. I breathed a sigh of relief and sat down on the floor, and my sister knelt down with me. Even when she was standing, she was already very small, but when she sat down, her presence became even more insignificant. Perhaps it was because she always kept her head down, like a crumpled piece of chewing gum wrapper, easily overlooked if not carefully observed.

I glanced back and forth between the diary placed between us and my dejected younger sister. Aside from the time Mom brought her home as a newborn, this was the first time I'd truly looked at my sister. The frail-looking girl I'd sensed then was still there; she'd only grown a little bigger. Her long, black hair hung down her cheeks like drooping rabbit ears.

If I ignored her, tears seemed about to well up in my eyes immediately. Seeing that look in my eyes, I naturally felt uncomfortable, as if I myself was about to feel depressed. I wanted to escape the depressing scene, but my body wouldn't move.

I'm not a particularly responsible person; normally, I would have already slipped away.

Who cares if you live or die? And I would think that way too.

But I can't do it now. Looking at my sister, I know it's impossible.

Why? I don't know the reasons or causes, but—

I can't not help her. I have that feeling.

Is it an instinctive sense of companionship inherent in living beings? Or is it because we are connected by blood? In any case, that thing is engraved in my body like a product serial number, demanding my help in a part of me that I cannot change, making it difficult for me to resist. Perhaps, once I become aware of that thing, I will only have one path left: to become the "older brother."

I picked up my drawing diary and read through the first three pages. My mother was the central figure in every drawing. Written in hiragana, the diary was difficult to read. It recorded events at home—or rather, chores my mother had done—in just a few words. "Mom did ○○." "Mom did ○○." The sentences were all the same. And there were no reflections on these events. I had kept this diary for three days straight.

As for me, I appeared in the picture frame the next day, but in the right corner, with only half my face showing. Although it's hard to tell from the picture whether it's me or not, I'm the only one in the whole family who would be treated so carelessly. That day's diary didn't mention me at all; it was simply because I happened to be in my sister's field of vision, so I was included in the drawing. This picture quite accurately depicts the relationship between my sister and me.

The diary entries were completely blank; I only wrote for three days before stopping. Since the diary only recorded things at home and didn't mention any outdoor activities, it felt like I wrote about the same things for three days before finally running out of ideas. I looked closely at my sister; unlike me, her skin showed no signs of sunburn. Did that mean she never goes out? Does she never go swimming in the school pool? Come to think of it, I realized I've never gone out with her. So, the reason the drawing diary is almost blank might not be because I'm lazy, but because I have no subject to write about.

"Don't you have any friends?"

I asked rashly. Hearing this, my sister's lips and cheeks twitched again. "Don't cry, don't cry," I frantically tried to comfort her again. My sister tried hard to hold back her tears, but snot still dripped from her nose. I took a tissue from the tissue box and wiped her nose. My sister didn't move and let me do whatever I wanted.

What a nuisance. To be honest, I find him a bit annoying.

However, I also know very well that I can't just leave her unattended.

"I'll help you."

After saying that, the younger sister immediately raised her head, and the tears that had been hanging in the corners of her eyes disappeared.

Perhaps it's because her hair is no longer covering her face, and the shadows on her face have faded, that even her expression seems more vibrant. What an easy-going guy.

But there's no need to call for help at such a critical moment. Now it's my turn to hold my head in my hands, racking my brains.

Writing nearly forty days' worth of diary entries in one go is no easy feat. But what made me scream in despair was the weather section below the dates. While the teacher probably couldn't remember the weather every day, piecing it together with other people's diaries would give me away. Of course, we didn't have a month-old newspaper at home, so I couldn't do any research.

I thought for a moment and decided to give up on filling in the weather information. "Just draw a smiley face or a sad face randomly on it," I instructed my younger sister instead. Not everyone likes sunny days, and some people even like rainy days. Since everyone's feelings are different, my sister can interpret whatever she thinks about the weather that day. As for the diary entry, I'll try to avoid mentioning the weather and just write whatever comes to mind.

If I were to write it for her myself, her handwriting would definitely give it away, and her level of Chinese character knowledge is different from mine. So, my younger sister will write the diary herself, and I'll be responsible for the illustrations. Although the brushstrokes shouldn't be too similar to the illustrations she drew in the previous three days, if I redid those too, the entire illustrated diary would be fabricated. That wouldn't be a diary anymore, just a mere fantasy picture book.

But even when I told my sister to make up whatever she wanted, she just looked at me with a tearful expression. "There's nothing to write about," she said weakly. Sure enough, that was it. She hadn't continued writing because she had nothing to write about. "Just make it up," I said, but my sister still seemed unable to think of anything to make up, her eyes trembling slightly, and her nostrils flaring a little. Did this mean I had to come up with the entire diary entry myself? I felt like I was about to faint.

I crossed my arms and stared at the wall. Although the number of cicadas had decreased, I could still hear them chirping in the house.

"There's really no other way... Well then, make up some stories about playing with me."

My sister nodded repeatedly, waiting for me to continue. Did I have to make up a whole story from the first sentence to the last? This was a much bigger undertaking than I'd imagined. I crossed my legs, my feet shaking up and down incessantly.

My sister's constant staring made me very uncomfortable. I tried to buy some time.

"Let me think about it. You draw the weather first."

If it's something this simple, even my younger sister should be able to do it. My sister nodded gently and began to draw. She quickly drew a smiley face. Her pen grip was ordinary, but her strength was remarkable, and the lines of the smiley face she drew were quite deep.

Its wide-open mouth is about as wide as Totoro's when it smiles.

Next, I drew a crying face. The drooping corners of its eyes were exactly the same as the little sister's from before.

In other words, even though I haven't seen it, if my sister smiles, her smile will actually look similar to that smiling face, right?

My dear sister, is it appropriate for a girl to laugh like that? I couldn't help but feel troubled.

Enough with the nonsense.

The situation seems to have devolved into me having to extract the contents of my daily diary and draw pictures. As a result, not only today, but also tomorrow and the day after, the little bit of summer vacation left will be wasted.

I feel like I've suffered a huge loss because my summer vacation ended early.

As for her younger sister, she was drawing emojis with practiced ease, but she probably wasn't thinking about anything at all. The proof is that she drew a smiley face on today's weather chart.

It really is just random scribbling. Wasn't it someone who came to me crying for help? I thought to myself, dumbfounded.

I looked out the window in the corridor and saw sunlight filtering through thin clouds, baking the rooftops of the houses across the street.

Although summer vacation is over, it seems that summer will continue for a while longer.

Holidays, as numerous as the pool water in a swimming pool, evaporate in the blink of an eye, just like any other summer vacation. But this year, as summer vacation was drawing to a close—though that might be a bit of an exaggeration—a younger sister suddenly appeared. And vice versa. In her heart, this was probably the first time she'd ever seen me as a "brother," right? Just as meat on a supermarket freezer is merely a commodity to someone who doesn't want to buy meat, but a necessary "ingredient" for someone who wants to make hamburger patties. Values determine the meaning of things.

Something extremely tiny, born between my sister and me, became the beginning of everything.

I silently stayed by my sister's side, watching her finish drawing the weather expressions.

Is this what they call an older brother? I feel a little uneasy and restless.

And is this what they call a younger sister? I stared intently at what was in front of me, thinking to myself.

Two weeks after summer vacation ended, my younger sister was still sitting in my room. This room had been converted from my own to a children's room, so it was only natural that she was sitting there. When I returned home after the opening ceremony, her desk and bed had already been moved into the room; I didn't even have a chance to object.

Was it a forceful decision by my parents? Or did my sister agree? The truth is unclear. Regardless, I had no say in this matter. My sister is always given priority in the family, while I'm relegated to a secondary position. But I don't think it's unfair.

Because my sister is a much more troublesome person than me. I've come to realize that.

I recalled the hard work of creating the illustrated diary. It took me about three days to piece together the entire summer's diary entries. By the end, even I was so exhausted from racking my brains for topics that my memory of those days was hazy. After all, my sister's fair skin made it impossible for her to participate in any outdoor activities, like swimming, so the topics I could create were naturally limited to indoors, or more specifically, to our home. I had to write about one activity at home every day, and draw a picture... By the end, I was almost mentally exhausted. Fortunately, so far, my sister's teacher hasn't been angry about her diary, so I guess I've managed to get away with it. If even a painstakingly crafted diary could make the teacher angry, I probably would have wanted to cry.

After school, my younger sister put her schoolbag on the table and sat in her chair, doing nothing, lost in thought. Since I started paying closer attention to her, I've finally noticed her strange posture: her legs are pressed together, forming a small mountain shape on the chair; her arms are wrapped around her knees, her palms between the chair seat and the soles of her feet. Whenever her hands move, her body sways slightly back and forth. It's very much like one of those roly-poly toy travel souvenirs I received from someone.

My sister noticed my gaze and turned her head:

"What's wrong, brother?"

The sound that makes my ears itch no matter how many times I listen to it.

"Um—it's nothing," I mumbled, resting my elbow on the table and propping my cheek up.

Ever since I started helping my younger sister with her summer homework, she's called me that. Before that, we barely spoke, let alone knew how to address each other. Compared to then, our interactions now are more like those of siblings. More like siblings. Since my parents look relieved after seeing our interactions, I think this is probably the type of siblings they hoped for. I can only use that as a benchmark.

But we only became "brother—" and "sister," not playmates. Even living in the same room, we didn't talk much. To me, my sister was like a giant bubble floating in mid-air. In other words, a foreign object. Soft, moist, but alien.

My younger sister was still lost in thought, looking like a marine iguana basking in the sun, completely unguarded, which made me increasingly worried. To be honest, I was afraid that nothing above her neck was moving.

I'm doomed if they ask me to help them with their homework again. I tentatively asked:

"Aren't you going to do your homework?"

"I'll write it later."

She glanced at me and said, "I didn't help with any of my summer homework besides the drawing journal, but my sister's teacher didn't seem angry. She said she should have finished all her other assignments obediently and wasn't a lazy kid who didn't do her homework. That reassured me a little. But if things continue like this, next summer might be the same again. Our school requires first and second graders to write drawing journals during the summer vacation, and I have a feeling it's unavoidable. Looking at my sister's carefree attitude, it seems impossible to expect her to grow into a diligent child who can automatically finish her journal within just one year."

I glanced at my sister's profile, then at the clock. The ticking of the hands was louder than anything we could make. There was still some time before dinner, so I decided to finish my homework first. Although I had no plans for tonight, I felt really annoyed if something I suddenly wanted to do came up, but I couldn't do it because I hadn't finished my homework. I'm the kind of person who's always thinking ahead about these little things; I'm not very magnanimous.

However, many adults mistakenly thought I was a conscientious and responsible child, which unexpectedly led to a fairly good reputation among adults. Although it was a misunderstanding, since I was overestimated, there's no need to change their opinions. Anyway, as far as the results matter, it's a fact that I always finish my homework early.

I started doing my Chinese exercises, and my younger sister, who had been daydreaming, faced the table, put her feet down, straightened her back, and adopted a normal sitting posture. Then she took out her blue math workbook from her schoolbag, which had been tossed on the table, and began doing her homework.

She was still holding the pen with the same force, so the pen pressure must be quite strong. I glanced at my sister writing and thought to myself.

"Weren't you going to write it later?"

"I'm imitating my older brother—"

My younger sister answered without taking her eyes off her workbook. I thought for a moment about what she was talking about.

"Why?"

"Because my brother—is very good at writing diaries."

My sister's answer was quite brief and lacked explanation, but it was still clear from it that she had reflected on her diary. She started imitating me so she could write a whole diary like me. But what I wrote was more like a picture book than a diary. Good children shouldn't imitate that.

"I think if the older brother's level rises, it might be possible to write about it."

What is that? My sister seems to have a set of standards I've never heard of before, but when she suddenly voiced them, I was left speechless. Come to think of it, what's the point of a sister raising her brother's standards? Shouldn't it be the sister's standards that need raising?

Although I don't understand my sister's thinking, she certainly has the desire to improve herself. Striving to overcome failure or achieve what was previously impossible is a positive attitude and deserves encouragement. While that's my opinion, I also feel that her efforts might be heading in the wrong direction.

Well, since they're learning from me and finishing their homework early, it shouldn't be a bad thing.

After doing my homework for a while, I got up to go to the bathroom. My younger sister also looked up, got up, and walked behind me. "No way?" I thought to myself as I started walking, and she actually followed, even mimicking my walk.

"I don't think there's any point in doing this."

"Just do it first and talk about it later."

My sister replied verbally, her body still mimicking my actions. Her eyes were fixed on me; was she driven by action? Or stubborn? Or impulsive and reckless? Which was it? I began to wonder.

We went downstairs one after the other. My younger sister wanted to follow me into the restroom, but I stopped her from going in. After using the restroom, my sister washed her hands with me, just like I did.

"There's no point in doing this, is there?"

"It's so cool. It feels so good."

My sister's mood soared like splashing water. It didn't really matter, but the way she dried her hands was too haphazard, so I had to take her hands and help her dry them. Just then, my mother walked by and saw our interaction. I felt like my eyelashes were so heavy they were about to pull my eyelids down.

This must be what they call being embarrassed or awkward. Probably.

She walked towards me, carrying her Mandarin textbook and reading cards.

"Oh, it's Chinese homework? Okay."

It seems I used to read aloud to my mother, but today it seems I can do it too. My younger sister sat directly under the light in the center of the room, and I sat opposite her. After she opened her textbook, she remained still for a while.

"What's wrong?"

"Brother—you read first."

As she spoke, she handed me the textbook. Why? I understood before I even asked.

Is this considered having one's own opinions, or lacking them?

It's good to see things through to the end, but if this continues, will my little sister be okay by next year? I'm quite worried.

I will also be affected, but it's not exaggerated to the point that it will affect my future.

Even so, I think about my sister's future, so I guess I'm pretty much a brotherly brother. By the way, what exactly is "brotherly brotherly"?

Compared to my brother, Du, which one is more decent? I couldn't help but ponder.

"Brother—what is it—?"

Her way of speaking seems to have made no progress at all. The fact that I think this means I've gotten used to her way of speaking.

My sister flinched and silently handed me her diary. "Wait," I said, holding out my hand to stop her.

We need to take preventative measures before she cries for help.

My younger sister's problem is that she lacks topics for her diary. Since that's the case, she just needs to create her own. I gave her a random example, but she tilted her head:

"Observe what?"

"If you want to observe something, how about a sunflower? If it's a plant, you can write about it even if you haven't observed it carefully."

Why would you think that? I'm not going to write your diary for you. I looked away.

That seems to be the case. She probably wants me to pick her up and drop her off.

Since I asked her first, it was hard for me to refuse her request.

It's probably because I'm not used to seeing that kind of scene, and besides, my sister rarely goes out. As for myself, I've never taken her out except to school with her.

My younger sister came to the entryway carrying a diary, drawing supplies, and an umbrella. The umbrella was white on the outside and black on the inside, so it looked like a parasol. Isn't it a bit over the top to bring all this equipment just to school?

Just like her mother. Perhaps it's precisely because she imitates her mother that she doesn't want to be in the sun.

"This is how you achieve beautiful skin—"

My younger sister answered in a flat tone. She must have learned that from someone.

My sister and I walked in the shade created by the parasol. My sister can't ride a bicycle, and our parents don't allow us to carry each other, so we had to walk. But it only takes three minutes to get to school by bike, and five minutes to walk, so there's not much difference between the two.

In the flowerbeds of other classes, there were dried loofahs and various flowers that had withered from the heat. The soil was covered with petals of all sizes, a dazzling sight. Where there are many plants, there are naturally many insects. Although I'm not afraid of most insects, I'm not so sure about bees. I think being stung would hurt a lot. My younger sister didn't seem to see the bees; she calmly took out her diary-writing supplies. But she has to write in her drawing diary every day, which means I have to come with her every day? Even though she writes the diary herself, accompanying her to school every day is still a hassle. I should have talked about the flowers in our yard instead of sunflowers. I'm starting to regret it.

However, I don't know the name of the flowers that bloom in my yard.

I held the parasol for my sister. "Brother—you're so tall—" she exclaimed, looking up. Was she referring to my height? It didn't feel bad to be told I was tall.
